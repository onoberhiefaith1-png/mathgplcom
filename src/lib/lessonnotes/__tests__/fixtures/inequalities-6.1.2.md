### V5. Expert/JAMB-style

Question V5

Find all real \\(k\\) for which the solution set of \\(-2x+k<6\\) is \\(x>-1\\).

Solution

\\(-2x+k<6\\)

Subtract \\(k\\) from both sides:

\\(-2x<6-k\\)

Divide both sides by −2 and reverse the inequality sign:

\\(x>\frac{k-6}{2}\\)

The required solution set is \\(x>-1\\).

Therefore, the lower boundaries must be equal:

\\(\frac{k-6}{2}=-1\\)

Multiply both sides by 2:

\\(k-6=-2\\)

Add 6 to both sides:

\\(\boxed{k=4}\\)

### Question 4

Classify the solutions of \\((a-2)x>6\\) for all real \\(a\\).

Solution

\\((a-2)x>6\\)

Consider three cases.

Case 1: \\(a>2\\)

Then \\(a-2>0\\).

Divide both sides by the positive number \\(a-2\\):

\\(\boxed{x>\frac{6}{a-2}}\\)

Case 2: \\(a<2\\)

Then \\(a-2<0\\).

Divide both sides by the negative number \\(a-2\\) and reverse the inequality sign:

\\(\boxed{x<\frac{6}{a-2}}\\)

Case 3: \\(a=2\\)

Substitute \\(a=2\\):

\\((2-2)x>6\\)

\\(0>6\\)

This is false, so there is no solution.

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{cases} x>\dfrac{6}{a-2},&a>2,\\\\[5pt] x<\dfrac{6}{a-2},&a<2,\\\\[5pt] \varnothing,&a=2. \end{cases}} \\]

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{cases} x\le1,&a>0,\\\ x\ge1,&a<0,\\\ x\in\mathbb R,&a=0. \end{cases}} \\]

### Question 11

Explain why multiplying \\(x<2\\) by \\(x\\) requires separating cases by the sign of \\(x\\).

Solution

Multiplying an inequality by a positive number preserves its direction, but multiplying by a negative number reverses it.

Consider three cases.

Case 1: \\(x>0\\)

Multiply \\(x<2\\) by positive \\(x\\):

\\(x^2<2x\\)

Case 2: \\(x<0\\)

Multiply \\(x<2\\) by negative \\(x\\) and reverse the inequality sign:

\\(x^2>2x\\)

Case 3: \\(x=0\\)

Multiplying by zero gives:

\\(0=0\\)

The strict inequality becomes an equality.

Therefore, multiplying by \\(x\\) requires knowing whether \\(x\\) is positive, negative or zero.
