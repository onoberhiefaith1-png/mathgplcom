# Math Lab — Stage 14: Explore / Step-by-Step / Show Solution

Stage 13 gave Math Lab one input box that recognises any typed maths. Stage 14 makes every result *teach*: three learning modes on every answer, produced entirely by the engine's own calculations — no AI, no credits, works offline.

## The three modes

- **Explore** — a guiding question appears first ("What does it mean when two lines meet?"). The student drags points and inspects the graph before any answer is revealed.
- **Step-by-Step** — one justified micro-step per line, in the classroom style, revealed one at a time with a Next button.
- **Show Solution** — the full answer plus the matching picture, immediately.

## First two families (this stage)

1. **Simultaneous equations** — e.g. `2x = y + 3; x + y = 6` → (3, 3). Steps: rearrange, substitute, solve, check. Detects and explains parallel lines (no solution) and coincident lines (infinitely many) when coefficients change.
2. **Quadratics with transformations** — vertex form y = a(x − h)² + k with sliders for a, h, k; steps connect the sliders to the graph: direction, vertex, axis of symmetry, roots.

## Significance

Math Lab stops being a plotter and becomes a teacher. A student who types two equations doesn't just see where they cross — they are asked what the crossing *means*, then shown each algebraic step that finds it, then the full solution with the picture. A teacher can project the Step-by-Step mode and reveal lines one at a time, exactly as on a whiteboard. This beats the traditional method because the picture, the algebra and the reasoning stay linked: move a slider and the steps' numbers update to match.
