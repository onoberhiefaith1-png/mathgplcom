# Create Building (staff) and Choose Building (everyone else)

The Building layer keeps everything it has today — the existing building, its settings, rooms, doors, frames, windows, screens, lighting. What changes is the way in there, and how quickly a new building can be made.

The build page's Building entry becomes role-aware:

- **Site owner, administrator, building manager** see **Create Building**: the existing building opens in a distraction-free full-screen workspace with only the building and its settings, and one **Save** at the bottom that asks for a name and a category and files the result in the MATHGPL Building Gallery as a brand-new building. The original is never touched.
- **Everyone else eligible** sees **Choose Building**: categories first, then one building at a time in the live walkable preview, with swipe and arrows, and **Use Building** to bring the complete package into their own space. They can then change it privately, and optionally **Share with Community**.

## Faster images: two master pictures instead of sixteen

The building still paints 16 picture positions — 8 upper (outer) and 8 lower (inner). The first time the pictures are edited, only two inputs are shown: **Outer Building Image** and **Inner Building Image**. Each can be uploaded or taken from MATHGPL Assets, has background removal and a preview, and **Continue** copies the outer picture into all eight upper positions and the inner picture into all eight lower positions.

After Continue the familiar 8 + 8 grid appears, and every position stays independently editable — changing position 4 changes nothing else. The two master pictures are only a fast starting point.

## The current building becomes Gallery Building #1

The building that exists today is published into the gallery as the first entry, so it can be picked, named, filed under a category, and used as the starting template for the next building. Saved buildings keep their name, category, all sixteen pictures, structure, rooms, doors, windows, frames, screens, lighting and effects across sign-outs.
