# Pictures really go inside frames and windows — and everything can be resized

Two jobs, both confirmed against the running building.

## 1. The picture goes in and is actually seen

The picture is already being saved — the newest window in your building has an
uploaded picture recorded against it. The problem is where the picture is placed
in the 3D object:

- In a **window**, the picture is set *behind* the wall opening, and the
  window's own solid back panel sits in front of it. So the picture is there,
  but hidden — you see the empty pane instead.
- In a **frame**, the picture sits a fraction of a millimetre in front of the
  frame's back panel, so the two surfaces fight for the same spot and the
  picture flickers away or shows as a dull board.

Fix: the picture becomes the visible inside surface of both objects. The frame's
back panel only shows when there is no picture. In a window, the picture is the
view seen through the glass, lit and set inside the opening but never behind the
wall. Both keep their 3D surround exactly as designed, and both still blend
naturally as you slide the camera left and right.

## 2. Size, shape and position for all three

- **Frames and windows** keep their existing width, shape (Portrait / Square /
  Landscape) and position controls, but they will now be easy to reach: clicking
  the object in the building opens its own controls straight away, already
  scrolled to it.
- **The TV / Smart Screen** currently has a fixed size worked out from the room
  and no controls at all. It gains the same set: bigger / smaller, Portrait /
  Square / Landscape (widescreen stays the default), move it across the wall and
  raise or lower it. Each room remembers its own screen size.

## 3. Adding a picture from this page

Upload from your own device becomes the main action on a frame, a window and the
screen — one tap, choose the file, done. Browsing your MathGPL library stays
available as a smaller second option for when you want a picture already stored
there; it is no longer the first thing you see.
