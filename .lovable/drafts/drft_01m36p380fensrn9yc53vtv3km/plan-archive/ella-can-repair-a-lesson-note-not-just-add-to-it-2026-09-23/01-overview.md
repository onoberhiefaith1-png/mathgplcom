# Ella can repair a lesson note, not just add to it

Today Ella can create a note, add a session, append lines, highlight a solution,
generate Floating Numbers, open a Smartboard dry run and assign work. What she
cannot do is touch anything that already exists: she cannot rename a title, fix a
solution line, move an example out of an Introduction and into its own session,
reorder anything, or delete a leftover duplicate. So when a lesson is badly
arranged her only move is to append a corrected copy and leave the mess behind.

This plan gives her the missing hands — editing, moving, reordering, removing —
with a safety net so a wrong move can be undone, and honest reporting so she
never claims a result she did not read back.

## What she will be able to do

- Read a whole notebook as a structure: sections, sessions, question and solution
  lines, and what is missing from each.
- Change existing text: note title, session title, a question line, a solution line.
- Move content: a line into another session, a whole question into its own new
  session, a session to a different place in the note.
- Reorder lines, sessions and sections.
- Remove a duplicate line or an empty leftover session — only after she has
  shown you exactly what will go and you have said yes.
- Take a restore point before any repair and put the note back if the repair
  goes wrong.
- Read back the saved note after every change and report only what she read.

## Rules she must keep

- The teacher's question text is untouchable unless the teacher asks for it to
  change. A move never rewrites mathematics; it carries the same text across.
- A question lives in a real session, so the Smartboard can step through it.
- Floating Numbers stay tied to the solution they came from: if a solution line
  moves or changes, she says the chips need regenerating rather than pretending
  they still match.
- Every removal, and anything that reaches students, is confirmed first.
- She acts as you, inside your workspace, under the same permissions the screens
  use. No wider access, no keys, no other people's material.
